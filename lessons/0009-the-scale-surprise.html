Lesson 0009

The scale surprise

Same algorithm A, same training objective, wildly bigger D — and something happens that no one explicitly asked for.

At small scale, next-word prediction looks like autocomplete. At huge scale, the same objective begins to produce translation, coding, summarization, conversation, and reasoning-like behavior. The model did not get a new goal; scale changed what the goal could express.

The training objective never moved

Lesson 0007's table already told you what GPT is: the transformer family, run through D --A--> f^_D, with A = predict-the-next-word. That's the whole objective — the same one whether D is a small pile of text or the trillion-plus-word piles from Lesson 0008. Nobody added a "write working code" objective, or a "translate French" objective. A never changed. What changed was the size of D — and, as D grew by orders of magnitude, the fitted model's behavior stopped looking like autocomplete and started looking like something else.

Same skill, bigger context to use it in

Predicting the next word plausibly requires modeling a lot more than grammar, once the training text is varied enough. To guess the next token after "The French word for 'cat' is" well, a model has to have picked up something that functions like translation. To guess the next token in the middle of working Python, it has to have picked up something that functions like completing code. None of these were named as separate goals — they're all still "predict the next token," just applied to text varied and voluminous enough that predicting it well requires those skills as a byproduct.

Sharpen your pencil: objective, or byproduct?

Sort each capability: was it the model's explicit training objective, or a byproduct of getting good at that one objective over huge, varied text?

📈
The recurring idea, again

A model can only express what its shape and its training allow (Lesson 0005's lesson, restated). Scale doesn't change the shape or the objective — it changes what satisfying that same objective, at that size, turns out to require.

This isn't magic, and it isn't reliable

"Reasoning-like behavior" is a careful phrase, not a claim that the model reasons the way a person does. Lesson 0007's Box quote still applies at any scale: the model is still wrong in specific, sometimes surprising ways — confidently wrong, not just occasionally wrong — because nothing about scaling up next-word prediction gives the model a way to check its guesses against reality. Bigger D changes what the model can produce. It doesn't change what kind of thing the model is.
