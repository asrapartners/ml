Homework 1: the map

Four required problems, one optional bonus, all built from pieces you've already met. This lesson is the map — it doesn't teach anything new by itself, it shows you where each problem sits relative to lessons 0001–0010, so nothing on the page feels unfamiliar before you open it.

🧭
The pattern across all five problems

Every problem hands you a fitted score and asks two things: compute a small quantity from it, then explain what that quantity does — and does not — establish about the real-world decision. That's the x → f^_D → s → δ → a pipeline from Lesson 0001, wearing different clothes each time.

The math notation you'll actually see

Homework 1 introduces a small batch of new symbols on top of the ones in the notation decoder. None of them are conceptually new — they're the same "estimate vs. truth," "which batch of data," and "map a raw score to something usable" ideas, just written for this specific setting.

How to use these lessons

Every interactive widget in lessons 0012–0016 uses different numbers than the actual homework — same structure, different data. That's deliberate: the syllabus says GenAI tools can't do the assignment's central reasoning for you, so these lessons build the skill on a parallel example, and you apply it yourself to Homework 1's actual tables. If a widget's mechanics feel automatic, that's the point — the only thing left for the real homework is plugging in its numbers and writing the explanation in your own words.

Want to skip straight to one problem, or start at 0012 and go in order? Either works — just ask your teaching agent.
